#+title: 

Let \(X\) be a [[id:7D0EC5C1-73C1-4CA7-9B4F-B017307F8B72][compact]] pretopological space.  For any family \((C_i)_{i \in I}\) of closed subsets of \(X\) such that \(\bigcap_{i \in I} C_i = \emptyset\), there exists a finite subset \(J \subseteq I\) such that \(\bigcap_{i \in J} C_i = \emptyset\).  Taken complements, this shows that any open cover of \(X\) has a finite subcover.

* proof

Suppose, on the contrary, that each finite subset \(J \subseteq I\) has \(\bigcap_{i \in J} C_i \neq \emptyset\).  Then
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{F} = \Set{ \bigcap_{i \in J} C_i | J \subseteq I \land \left| J \right| < \aleph_0 }
\end{equation*}
is trivially a filter base on \(X\).  Let \(x\) be a [[id:65DDF6EF-9630-4E14-86AF-E7821FA78A64][cluster point]] of \(\mathcal{F}\).  Then \(x\) is also a cluster point of each \(C_i\) ([[id:238FB567-C426-4797-9756-A2444D791A81][why]]), so \(x \in C_i\) for all \(i \in I\) ([[id:126F40D8-D7A0-43F5-8187-AB49582483C8][why]]).  But \(\bigcap_{i \in I} C_i\) is empty, contradiction.  \(\square\)

* reference

Generalising
- Boubaki, General Topology, Chapter 1, § 9, no. 1, pp. 83--84.

* converse not true

The converse statement is not true: Let \(X'\) be the topological space induced by \(X\).  Then \(X'\) being compact does not imply that \(X\) is compact.  Consider \(\mathbb{Z}\) equipped with a pretopological structure: each \(\mathcal{N}(x)\) is generated by \(\{x-1, x, x+1\}\).  Then the topology induced on \(\mathbb{Z}\) is trivial, thus compact, but \(\mathbb{Z}\) as a pretopological space is not compact, since the filter \(\{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq x} \mid x \in \mathbb{Z}\}\) has no cluster point.  \(\triangle\)

However, the converse is clearly true if \(X\) is a topological space.  Note that this does not characterise \(X\) being a topological space in any way.  Consider a finite pretopological space \(X\) that is not a topological space.  Then any subspace of \(X\) is finite, and thus compact both as a pretopological space and as a topology space.
